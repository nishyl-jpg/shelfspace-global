Business model

A decor service business with three lines that feed each other: home styling, newborn nurseries and events. It sells time and taste, not stock, so it can start on about R24,000 and earn in its first month.

1. Why three lines in one business

Each line is small on its own. Together they give you one customer several times over:

  • Nursery leads to events. Every nursery client has a baby shower, then a first birthday, then more birthdays.
  • Events lead to homes. Guests see your work in someone's house. Hosts ask about the lounge.
  • Homes smooth the calendar. Events peak October to March and on weekends. Home and nursery work fills weekdays and winter.

One brand, one set of tools, one Instagram account, three reasons to call you.

How these compare to the market. A published South African cost guide puts a professionally designed nursery at R5,200 to R14,000, around R9,600 typical. Client-facing decorator rates are quoted at R400 to R1,200 an hour. Your packages work out to about R300 to R420 an hour of your time, which is the lower-middle of that range: right for a new mid-market business. Published wedding decor spend runs R8,000 to R150,000; your Celebration package sits at the entry end on purpose.

Add-ons that lift the average job

What this tells you:

  • Home and nursery work is nearly all margin because you sell hours.
  • Events earn the least per rand because you rent in what you do not own. Every piece of hire stock you buy moves event margin up. That is the main use of investor money.
  • Your limit is hours. About 130 billable hours a month is a full diary for one person. Beyond that you need a second stylist.

Assumptions behind each case

  • Expected: 4 jobs a month in months 1 to 3, then 8, then 11, then 14 in the peak quarter. Overheads R6,500 a month: marketing R2,000, storage R1,500, accounting R850, non-job fuel R800, phone and data R500, insurance R450, software R400. You pay yourself R12,000 a month for six months, then R18,000.
  • Cautious: 5 jobs a month on average, more small jobs, less advertising. You draw R6,000 a month, so you need other income this year.
  • Strong: word of mouth and one or two trade partners deliver 13 jobs a month on average. Overheads include a part-time second stylist (R90,000) and more storage and advertising. You pay yourself R20,000 a month.

At R2.3 million turnover VAT registration becomes compulsory; none of these cases reach it. All overhead figures verify.

7. Break-even

With overheads of R6,500 a month and an average gross profit per job of R3,100 (R5,000 at 62%), you cover costs at 3 jobs a month. To also pay yourself R12,000 you need 6 jobs a month. To pay yourself R18,000 you need 8.

8. Where growth comes from

  1. Own the hire stock. Chairs, tables, linen and backdrops bought with investor money lift the Celebration margin from about 40% toward 60%, and can be hired out on their own.
  2. Trade accounts. Two estate agencies and three venues sending steady work is worth more than any advert.
  3. A second stylist once you pass 100 billable hours a month for three months running.
  4. Nursery kits. Pre-designed nursery looks sold as a shopping list and layout for R650: income without your hours.
