Legal requirements and your ground work

Decor is lightly regulated: no trade licence is needed to style a room or a party. What you do need is a properly registered company, clean paperwork with clients, insurance, and care around events and nurseries. Part A is what the law and good practice require. Part B is what you do, week by week.

Needs professional sign-off. This is researched guidance, not legal advice. Before you take investor money or sign your first client, have (1) an attorney review your client terms and any shareholder agreement, and (2) an accountant confirm your tax elections (turnover tax or normal company tax). One meeting with each is enough at this stage.

A2. Dealing with clients

LawWhat it requires of youHow you comply
Consumer Protection ActPlain-language terms; itemised quotes; quality service; fair cancellation. A client may cancel an advance booking and you may keep only a reasonable charge, judged by notice given and whether you could rebook the date. No charge if the booking cannot be honoured because of death or hospitalisation.A one-page signed quote with a cancellation scale: over 30 days, deposit refunded less costs already spent; 8 to 30 days, 50% of the fee; 7 days or less, the full fee less unspent costs. Attorney to confirm the scale is reasonable.
Electronic Communications and Transactions ActA website that offers services must show who you are: full legal name, registration number, physical address, phone, email, prices, payment terms, and how to complain.Add the registration number and address to the site footer as soon as you have them. The space is ready in the site's settings block.
Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA)Collect only what you need, keep it safe, use it only for the purpose given, get consent for marketing messages and for posting photos of people or homes.Register yourself as Information Officer with the Information Regulator (free, online). Add a short privacy notice to the site. Keep photo consent in writing. Never add clients to broadcast lists without asking.
Advertising standardsClaims and before-and-after photos must be true and your own work.Only post your own projects. Label portfolio projects done for friends honestly.

A4. Rules specific to each service line

LineRuleWhat you do
HomeElectrical work must be done by a registered electrician who issues a certificate of compliance.You do not move plugs or wire lights. Subcontract and keep the certificate.
Building alterations need approved plans; home builders must be registered.Stay in styling: paint, panelling, fittings, furniture. Anything structural goes to a registered builder under their own contract with the client.
Sectional title and estate rules limit work hours and changes.Ask the client for the conduct rules before a job in a complex.

Use low-odour, water-based paint and finish two weeks before the due date. Follow the cot maker's assembly instructions and keep them.

Part B · Your ground work, week by week

Starts Monday 16 November 2026. First paid jobs from week 5; full launch in week 7, the first week of January.

Week 1 · Make it official

  • Register on BizPortal; reserve Mooimaak plus two back-up names; file the company registration (R175).
  • Register mooimaak.co.za and set up hello@mooimaak.co.za.
  • Create all social accounts (steps in the content playbook) to secure the handle.
  • Buy a SIM for the business number; install WhatsApp Business.
  • Send me the number, email and handle, or put them into the settings block at the bottom of the website file.
  • Phone five decor and styling businesses as a customer and write down their prices for a baby shower for 30, a nursery, and a lounge refresh.

Week 2 · Money and cover

  • Open the business bank account with the registration documents.
  • Get three public liability quotes from brokers; take the cover.
  • Book one hour with an accountant: tax regime, bookkeeping app, how to pay yourself.
  • Book one hour with an attorney: review the quote and booking terms.
  • Register as Information Officer with the Information Regulator.
  • Adjust your price list using last week's competitor quotes.

Week 3 · Suppliers and kit

  • Open accounts or get price lists from: two decor hire companies, a balloon wholesaler, a flower market trader, a fabric shop, a paint shop, two baby furniture retailers.
  • Buy the starter event kit and tools (R11,500 budget). Buy second-hand where safe.
  • Find one reliable assistant; agree the day rate in writing.
  • Order business cards and two shirts.

Week 4 · Build the portfolio

  • Do three projects for friends or family at cost: one room, one nursery corner, one party table.
  • Film each one using the five-step method in the content playbook.
  • Get a written review and photo permission from each.
  • Replace the six placeholder tiles on the website with real photos.

Week 5 · Soft launch (14 December)

  • Publish the website on its own domain.
  • Post launch posts 1 to 4; announce the founding client offer.
  • Message 50 people you know personally with the link. Ask each for one introduction.
  • Take any December party or Christmas table job you can get, even small ones, for the photos.

Week 6 · Partners

  • Visit five antenatal instructors and three baby shops.
  • Visit five estate agencies; offer the half-price first staging day.
  • Set up the Google Business Profile and ask your three portfolio clients for reviews.

Week 7 · Full launch (first week of January)

  • Start the weekly posting rhythm.
  • Answer every enquiry within one working day; send every quote on the template.
  • Start a simple job log: date, client, package, price, costs, hours, where they found you.

Weeks 8 to 9 · Deliver and learn

  • Deliver the first founding-client jobs. Ask for a Google review on the day.
  • Compare actual hours to the hours in the business model; fix any package that runs over.
  • Visit five venues without in-house decor; ask to join their supplier lists.
  • Register for UIF and the Compensation Fund once your assistant passes 24 hours a month.

Weeks 10 to 11 · Turn on advertising

  • With 9 posts and 3 reviews in place, start the R2,000 a month ad plan.
  • File the trade mark search, then the applications.
  • Book a stand at one baby expo or market.

Week 12 · Review

  • Count: enquiries, bookings, revenue, costs, hours, reviews. Compare with the cautious and expected cases.
  • Decide whether to raise prices, drop a package, or add one.
  • If you have at least 12 paid jobs and 10 reviews, you have the proof needed to open the investor conversation.
