Ship in a Bottle · Document 1 of 4

Business model

Ship in a Bottle imports ready-to-drink cocktails that South Africans already know from social media but cannot buy here, and sells them online to the public and by the case to licensed bars, bottle stores and events. It is the most profitable of your three businesses if it works, and the slowest, most regulated and most capital-hungry to start.

Read this first. Three facts shape everything below.
  1. You may not sell or deliver a single drink until you hold a liquor licence. The website is built in waiting-list mode for that reason.
  2. BuzzBallz belongs to Sazerac, one of the largest spirits companies in the world, which bought the brand in 2024 and chooses its own distributor in each country. My searches found launches in the UK, Ireland, Mexico and India, and no South African distributor. That is an opening, but they are more likely to appoint an established liquor distributor than a start-up.
  3. The profit depends on your buying price. Buying from an overseas wholesaler leaves a thin margin. Buying at distributor prices leaves a healthy one. Section 4 shows both.

1. The opportunity

  • Ready-to-drink cocktails are one of the fastest-growing parts of the global drinks market, and BuzzBallz is the product people film: a round, colourful, 200 ml, 15% cocktail.
  • South Africans see these products daily on TikTok and Instagram and cannot buy them locally. That is demand created for free.
  • Local ready-to-drink products are mostly 4% to 7% coolers and ciders. A 15% single-serve cocktail is a different product at a different price.
  • The name works beyond one brand: Ship in a Bottle is "the drinks you've seen online, landed here". If one brand's supply closes, the shop carries others.

Higher-margin add-ons

  • Branded ice tubs and party packs (case, tub, cups): add R150 to R300 of margin per pack
  • Gift boxes of six with a card
  • Event bar packages for licensed event organisers
  • Other imported ready-to-drink brands on the same shipment, chosen for better buying terms
  • Later: your own contract-made cocktail under the Ship in a Bottle name, which is where the best margin is

Assumptions

  • Expected: sales grow from 800 units in month 1 to 3,000 in month 12; 55% to the public at an average of R80 excluding VAT, 45% to trade and events at R73.91.
  • Expected overheads: premises R84,000; marketing R60,000; part-time packer R54,000; card and payment fees R27,000; accounting with VAT and excise returns R18,000; insurance R12,000; breakage and losses R12,700; licence renewals R8,000; software R6,000. Public delivery is covered by the R60 fee.
  • Cautious: the novelty fades, a weaker rand lifts cost, and volume stays under 1,000 a month. The business loses money and you draw nothing.
  • Strong: an official distribution agreement cuts landed cost to about R45, bottle-store listings lift volume, and overheads grow with a van, a second staff member and more marketing.

7. Break-even

  • Route C, overheads about R23,500 a month, average gross profit R18 a unit: about 1,300 units a month to cover costs, and about 1,750 to also pay you R8,000.
  • Route A, same overheads, average gross profit R33 a unit: about 710 units a month to cover costs.

1,300 units a month is about 54 cases: for example 15 trade accounts taking two cases each, plus 150 online orders of a four-pack.

8. Risks and what to do about them

RiskHow likelyWhat you do
Licence is delayed or refusedMediumUse a specialist. Choose premises away from schools and places of worship. Investor money is released in two tranches so only stage 1 is at risk.
Brand owner appoints someone else, or objectsMedium to highApproach them first, with a licensed partner. Build the shop as multi-brand. Never present yourself as an official seller unless you are.
Rand weakensHighReprice each shipment. Keep at least two months of stock cover, not six.
Thin margin on route CCertainTreat route C as a bridge. Push add-ons and party packs. Negotiate volume pricing from the second shipment.
Sale to a minorLow, severeAge gate, ID check at the door on every delivery, written driver instructions, refusal log. This can cost you the licence.
Theft and breakageMediumAlarmed, caged storage. Stock count weekly. Insurance.
Advertising rules tightenMediumFollow the industry code now (see content playbook). Build an owned WhatsApp and email list, which a platform ban cannot remove.
Fad fadesMediumAdd new imported lines every quarter. The promise is "what's new from overseas", not one product.
