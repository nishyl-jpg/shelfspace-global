Content playbook

Groengeld has two audiences that are reached in different ways. Households find you in their suburb's WhatsApp and Facebook groups. Restaurants are won in person, at the back door, between lunch and dinner service. Social media supports both; it replaces neither.

2. Account setup, step by step

  1. WhatsApp Business on the business number. Profile: logo, "Standing fruit and veg orders, Cape Town", hours, website link.
  2. WhatsApp catalogue: the four boxes and five extras with prices and photos.
  3. Quick replies: /prices, /areas, /pause, /pay, /welcome. Labels: Household, Restaurant, New lead, Paused, Overdue.
  4. Create a WhatsApp Channel, "Groengeld: this week's box". Post the box list and one cooking idea every week.
  5. Two broadcast lists, opt-in only: Households (box list, day before delivery) and Kitchens (Sunday price list).
  6. Instagram Business and TikTok Business accounts. Bio: "Your fruit and veg on standing order. Bought at dawn, at your door by morning. Cape Town homes and kitchens. Build your order ↓".
  7. Instagram Highlights: How it works, This week's box, Kitchens, Reviews.
  8. Facebook Page linked to Instagram in Meta Business Suite.
  9. Google Business Profile as a service-area business; category "Fruit and vegetable wholesaler" with "Produce delivery" as a service.
  10. Join the community Facebook groups for every suburb on your website's area list. Note each group's advertising day.

Paid advertising

From week 9, R2,500 a month:

  • R1,500 on Facebook and Instagram ads aimed at people within 3 km of suburbs you already deliver in. Never advertise where you have no route.
  • R1,000 boosting your best market video with the button "Send WhatsApp message".
  • Measure cost per new standing order. A household is worth about R150 a week in gross profit, so paying up to R300 to win one is sound.

6. The restaurant visit, word for word

At the door, 14:30 to 16:30, never during service

"Hi, I'm [name] from Groengeld. I buy at the Epping market at dawn and deliver to kitchens around here before ten. I'm not here to sell you anything today. I've brought a sample crate and this week's price list. Who does your veg ordering?"

To the owner or chef

"What do you use most of each week? … And who brings it now? What goes wrong with them? … Here's what I'd charge for those five lines this week. Let me do one delivery free so you can see the quality. If it's not better than what you get now, you've lost nothing."

Follow-up by WhatsApp, next morning

"Morning Chef, [name] from Groengeld. Good to meet you yesterday. This week's prices on your five lines are below. I'm at the market Thursday: shall I bring your trial order? Just reply with quantities."

Keep a notebook: restaurant, name of the person who orders, what they use, who supplies them now, what annoys them. That last answer is your pitch next time.
