Legal requirements and your ground work

Selling whole fresh produce is one of the lighter-regulated food businesses, but it is still a food business. The essentials are a certificate of acceptability for wherever you handle and transport food, a legal scale, honest descriptions, and clean records. Part A is what is required. Part B is your week-by-week plan.

Needs professional sign-off. This is researched guidance, not legal advice. Before trading: (1) phone City Health's Environmental Health office for your area and confirm exactly what they need for home packing and for your vehicle; (2) have an accountant set up VAT and tax correctly, because this business will cross the VAT threshold in a good year; (3) have an attorney review your restaurant credit terms and any shareholder agreement.

What the health inspector looks for

  • A clean, pest-proof packing area with washable surfaces, separate from pets and household chemicals
  • Hand-washing facility with soap and paper towel
  • Crates and cooler boxes that are cleaned and stored off the floor
  • A vehicle load area that is clean, covered and used for nothing that could contaminate food
  • Produce kept off the ground and out of the sun
  • A simple cleaning schedule and your food-safety training certificate

Stay with whole, uncut produce. The moment you cut, peel, juice or make salads, you move into a stricter category with more requirements.

A3. Buying at the Cape Town Market

  • The market is at 110 Gunners Circle, Epping Industria (021 531 2191, info@ctmarket.co.za verify contact details).
  • The floor is cashless: you register for a buyer's card and load money onto it. Take your ID and company documents. Ask at the market office for the current process and any fees verify.
  • You buy from market agents, who sell on behalf of farmers. Agents are regulated by the Agricultural Produce Agents Council. Get to know two or three; a good agent will set quality aside for a regular buyer.
  • Keep every sales slip. They are your cost records and your traceability records.

Part B · Your ground work, week by week

Starts Monday 11 January 2027, once Mooimaak has launched. First deliveries in week 4. You commit two mornings a week, Tuesday and Thursday, finishing by 11:00.

Week 1 · Learn the market

  • Go to the Cape Town Market at 05:00 on two mornings. Walk every hall. Write down prices for 25 everyday items and the pack sizes they sell in.
  • Visit the market office: ask how to get a buyer's card and what it costs.
  • Talk to three agents. Ask what time the best stock goes and which days are cheapest.
  • Buy one Family Veg Box worth of produce at market prices. Work out the real cost. Compare with my R187 target.
  • Register the company and the domain; secure the social handles.

Week 2 · Paperwork

  • Open the bank account. Set up pay-by-link or EFT references.
  • Get a City of Cape Town business partner number and e-Services login.
  • Phone Environmental Health for your area: explain home packing of whole produce and vehicle delivery; ask what they want to see.
  • Email the business licensing office: does delivery of whole fresh produce need a licence?
  • Book and complete a short food-safety course.
  • Phone your car insurer about business use; get liability quotes.

Week 3 · Set up and test

  • Prepare the packing area to the inspector's checklist above. Submit the certificate of acceptability application.
  • Buy crates, cooler boxes, scale, boxes, trolley.
  • Update the website's price list in the settings block with real prices. Send me the number and email, or edit them in.
  • Do a full dry run for five friendly households: order, buy, pack, deliver, collect payment. Time every step.
  • Set up WhatsApp Business exactly as in the content playbook.

Week 4 · First real deliveries

  • Deliver to your first 10 founding households on Tuesday and Thursday.
  • Film the market and the deliveries. Post launch posts 1 to 4.
  • After each delivery morning, write down: time out, time back, drops, fuel, anything that went wrong.

Weeks 5 to 6 · First kitchens

  • Visit 10 restaurants a week between 14:30 and 16:30 using the script in the content playbook.
  • Do free trial deliveries for the interested ones. Have the attorney-reviewed account form ready.
  • Introduce yourself in community groups. Leaflet around every drop.
  • Check your real produce cost percentage. If it is above 58%, adjust box contents before adding customers.

Weeks 7 to 8 · Tighten the route

  • Group customers into two or three clusters. Stop accepting orders outside them for now.
  • Approach three complexes or estates with the five-box offer.
  • Start weekly invoicing discipline: invoice Friday, chase Monday, stop supply at 14 days.
  • Ask every customer past their third box for a Google review.

Weeks 9 to 10 · Institutions and ads

  • Visit creches, aftercares, guest houses and office canteens with a fixed weekly quote.
  • Start the R2,500 a month ad plan, only in suburbs you already serve.
  • Interview for a driver-packer. Do two paid trial mornings with the best candidate.

Weeks 11 to 12 · Decide on stage 2

  • Count: households, kitchens, weekly sales, produce cost %, credits %, drops per hour, overdue invoices.
  • If you have 30 or more households and 4 or more kitchens, hire the driver-packer and add Saturday.
  • Get quotes for a used bakkie with canopy and for a small cold-room unit near Epping. These go into the investor proposal as real figures.
  • Register for UIF and the Compensation Fund when you employ.

What only you can do

  • Be at the market at 05:00 and learn to judge produce by eye and hand
  • Win the first kitchens face to face
  • Meet the health inspector
  • Decide, at week 12, whether you or a hired driver does the mornings from then on
