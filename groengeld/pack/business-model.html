Business model

Groengeld delivers the same fruit and veg order to the same customers every week. Because orders are known in advance, it buys only what is already sold. That removes the two things that sink produce businesses: stock that rots and days with no sales.

1. The idea in plain terms

  • Standing order. A customer sets a list and delivery days once. It repeats until they change it.
  • Buy to order. On each delivery morning you buy exactly what tonight's order list adds up to, at the Cape Town Market in Epping, where anyone with a buyer's card can buy from the market agents.
  • Same-morning delivery. Produce goes from the market floor to the customer in hours, so you need no cold room at the start.
  • Two customer types on one route. Households pay better margins. Restaurants give volume. One vehicle serves both.

Who you compete with. For households: supermarket delivery apps and existing box schemes (published box prices in Cape Town run from about R145 to R680). You win on standing-order simplicity and market freshness, not on organic claims. For restaurants: the big produce wholesalers, who serve large accounts well and small ones badly. You win by being the supplier a small kitchen can reach at 19:45.

Assumptions

  • Expected: households average 15, 35, 60 then 80 across the four quarters, paying R360 a week. Restaurants average 2, 4, 7 then 10, spending R3,500 a week. Household margin 38%, restaurant margin 20% after delivery cost.
  • Expected overheads: driver-packer from month 4 (R6,500 a month), bakkie finance from month 4 (R5,500), cold-room unit from month 7 (R7,000), marketing R2,500, insurance R1,200, accounting R1,200, market fees and crates R1,500, phone and software R800, and 1.5% of restaurant sales lost to bad debts.
  • Cautious: half the customers, own car all year, a casual helper, no cold room. You draw R8,000 a month.
  • Strong: faster sign-ups, cold room from month 4, a second vehicle and driver from month 7.
  • Your pay in the expected case: R10,000 a month for six months, then R16,000.

All figures verify against your first eight weeks of real deliveries.

Tax point for your accountant. The expected and strong cases pass R2.3 million a year of sales, where VAT registration becomes compulsory. Fresh fruit, vegetables and eggs are zero-rated, so registering should not raise your produce prices, but delivery fees carry VAT and returns must be filed. Also ask about turnover tax: it taxes sales, not profit, which usually suits a low-margin, high-volume business badly. accountant to confirm

8. Break-even

  • Stage 1 (overheads about R7,500 a month): 8 households and 1 restaurant cover costs. 30 households and 3 restaurants leave about R20,000 a month for your pay.
  • Stage 3 (overheads about R26,000 a month): 30 households and 4 restaurants cover costs.
